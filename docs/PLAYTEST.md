# Beginner playtest kit

**Status: no peer sessions have been recorded yet.** Automated runs are not learning evidence. Do not fill this document with invented observations.

Invite 3 to 5 people with little or no coding experience. Ask whether they are happy to play and have anonymous notes recorded. Use P1, P2 and so on. No names, recordings or personal details are needed. Do not explain the lesson before they play.

## Before play

Ask: “If an AI agrees with past hiring decisions, does that mean it can spot capable people? Why?” Record their own words. Ask whether they have used machine learning before. Start a timer when they begin.

## During play

Let the person make their own choices. Note when they cannot find a control, misread a counter or confuse a label with a job check. Note whether they follow the tree and compare results after a change. Help only when they cannot continue, and record the help you gave.

At the receipt, stop the timer. Save the fictional session's JSON if the participant agrees. A high score is not proof that the person understood the concept.

## After play

1. Repeat the original question. Ask what changed their answer.
2. Ask: “What did Scout learn from? What is a label?”
3. Ask them to follow one CV through a real tree question.
4. Ask why more old hiring records might fail to help.
5. Ask: “A recycling AI learns from photos where each material has a different background colour. What photos would you collect to check whether it uses that shortcut?”
6. Ask which part was confusing and what one thing they would change.

## Review the evidence

Summarise how many participants completed the game, how many distinguished old choices from job checks, and how many proposed varied backgrounds for the recycling example. Give actual counts and examples, not claims about all beginners.

Choose at least one concrete improvement based on the observations. Record the observed problem, the change, and what happened when it was checked again. This feedback-driven revision is pending until real playtests happen.
